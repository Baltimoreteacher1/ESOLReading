Start Here: What This Book Is Really About

Three kids from different times are forced to leave home. They are not leaving for fun. They are leaving because staying could destroy their families.

Big idea: A refugee is a person forced to leave home because of danger, war, persecution, or disaster. The book shows that refugees are normal families in abnormal danger.
Reading move: For each chapter ask: What danger is growing? What choice does the family make? Who helps? Who refuses to help?

Josef’s Story: When Hate Becomes Law

Josef’s chapters show how quickly a child’s normal life can become unsafe when a government targets a group of people.

Germany • St. Louis • Cuba
🧳

Josef Landau

Simple summary: Josef is a Jewish boy in Nazi Germany. His father is taken to a concentration camp and returns changed. The family escapes on a ship, but escape is not simple because countries may refuse them.

Nazi dangership journeyforced maturity
1
Home becomes unsafe. Josef cannot trust police, neighbors, or laws. People in power treat Jewish families as if they do not belong.
2
Papa returns, but he is not okay. Papa has been traumatized. Josef begins to feel he must act older than his age.
3
The ship gives hope. The St. Louis feels like a way out, but it also becomes a floating question: Will anyone let them in?
4
Refusal hurts. Josef learns that danger is not only in Germany. It is also in the silence and rejection of other countries.

Josef: Easy Notes

Conflict: His family needs safety, but countries and governments block them.

Character change: Josef starts as a scared child and is pushed into adult responsibility.

Theme: A person can be harmed by both violence and refusal to help.

Remember: Josef’s story is not just about a boat. It is about being trapped between danger behind you and closed doors ahead of you.

Isabel’s Story: Hope Across Water

Isabel’s chapters show a family choosing danger at sea because staying home has also become dangerous.

Cuba • Homemade boat • Florida
🎺

Isabel Fernandez

Simple summary: Isabel loves music and family. Food shortages, political unrest, and fear push her family to escape Cuba on a homemade boat. The ocean becomes both a path and a threat.

musiccrossingsacrifice
1
Life in Cuba is unstable. Families struggle for basic needs. Isabel sees adults scared and angry.
2
The family chooses to leave. Leaving means danger, but staying may mean no future.
3
The boat is fragile. The family depends on each other, luck, and fast decisions.
4
Music becomes memory. Isabel’s trumpet is more than an object. It connects her to identity, culture, and home.

Isabel: Easy Notes

Conflict: She wants safety and a future, but the ocean is unpredictable and deadly.

Character change: Isabel learns that hope can require loss.

Theme: Home is not only a place. It is also family, memory, music, and culture.

Remember: Isabel’s story is full of sound: music, waves, engines, crying, shouting. Sound helps show fear and hope.

Mahmoud’s Story: Being Seen Can Save You

Mahmoud’s chapters show how war can make ordinary life disappear and how invisibility can be both protection and danger.

Syria • Turkey • Europe
👟

Mahmoud Bishara

Simple summary: Mahmoud lives through bombing and danger in Syria. He believes being invisible keeps him safe, but his journey teaches him that sometimes people must be seen so others will act.

warvisibilitysurvival
1
The city is no longer safe. Bombing and violence destroy the family’s normal life.
2
The family keeps moving. Mahmoud’s journey is not one trip. It is many risky steps.
3
The sea is terrifying. Like Josef and Isabel, Mahmoud faces water as a barrier between danger and safety.
4
Visibility changes meaning. Hiding can protect him from bullies or soldiers, but being noticed can bring help.

Mahmoud: Easy Notes

Conflict: His family needs safety, but borders, smugglers, police, water, and fear block the way.

Character change: Mahmoud moves from trying not to be seen to understanding the power of being seen.

Theme: The world often ignores suffering until people are forced to look.

Remember: Mahmoud’s story connects directly to modern news and real refugee experiences.

Page-Range Timeline: What to Remember

Use these checkpoints to review pages 1–252 without getting lost. Page numbers may shift by edition, so use them as guideposts.

Easy page map
pp. 1–50

Introductions: Three kids, three dangers

Josef faces Nazi Germany. Isabel faces unrest and shortages in Cuba. Mahmoud faces war in Syria. Each family begins to understand that home is no longer safe.

pp. 51–100

Families begin escape plans

The families make risky choices. They leave with little control over what happens next. Adults try to protect children, but children still see the fear.

pp. 101–150

The journey becomes dangerous

Water, borders, and people in power become major obstacles. The book keeps switching stories to show that refugee journeys repeat across history.

pp. 151–200

Hope and fear rise together

Every family gets close to safety, but each new step creates a new danger. Characters must decide what they will risk for family.

pp. 201–252

Choices, loss, and courage

The stories become more intense. Characters face impossible decisions. The reader sees that survival often depends on strangers, timing, sacrifice, and courage.

Themes and Symbols

A theme is a message the book teaches. A symbol is an object or image that means more than itself.

Big ideas
🚪

Closed Doors

Countries, borders, laws, and people can block refugees from safety. The book asks whether turning people away is another kind of harm.

Use this in writing: “The closed doors show that escaping danger is not enough if no one allows you in.”

👁️

Visibility vs. Invisibility

Mahmoud especially learns that being unseen may prevent attention, but it can also make suffering easier for others to ignore.

Use this in writing: “The book suggests that refugees need to be seen as real people, not problems.”

🌊

Water

The ocean appears in all three stories as hope and danger at the same time. It can carry people toward safety or threaten their lives.

Use this in writing: “Water symbolizes the dangerous line between home and safety.”

🎺

Music and Memory

Isabel’s music connects her to family, culture, and identity. Losing or protecting music shows what refugees carry inside them.

Use this in writing: “The trumpet represents Isabel’s identity and the home she carries with her.”

🤝

Who Helps?

In every story, survival depends on whether other people help, ignore, profit from, or reject refugees.

Use this in writing: “The author shows that small choices by strangers can change whether a family survives.”
